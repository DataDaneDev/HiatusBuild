This build is turning into a compact, serviceable off-grid workstation rather than a standard camper interior: the Hiatus shell is installed, the 48-volt Victron-based power architecture has already passed first-live checks, and the electrical core has moved from layout into a trimmed, freestanding 80/20 module with the MultiPlus, Cerbo, SmartShunt, Orion step-down, shore-power path, solar provisions, and future dedicated alternator integration all organized around a high-capacity battery bank. The real differentiator is the systems density: power, workstation ergonomics, galley, wet spine, battery storage, service access, and road restraint are being designed as interlocked modules instead of separate cabinets. The next phase is mechanical integration—tying the electrical tower into the bench, battery, and step structure, locking in the desk and monitor envelope, then building the galley module around the sink, faucet, induction cooktop, air-fryer cubby, tank, pump, and graywater path. Once those modules are braced, paneled, strain-relieved, labeled, and shakedown-tested, the build becomes a roadworthy mobile office with serious off-grid electrical capacity, maintainable service zones, and a much higher level of systems engineering than a typical DIY camper.
